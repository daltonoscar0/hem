# Decisions

A running log of engineering decisions made while building Hem, one line each,
including the ones that turned out to be wrong.

## The dial

- 2026-09-08: Hem reuses Mend's SwDA parser, clean-sentence pool, base model and
  training recipe unchanged, so the only difference between the two repos is the
  direction of the mapping. Anything that differs in the results is then
  attributable to the task rather than to the setup.
- 2026-09-08: Rates are counted in events per 100 **clean** words, not per
  spoken word. Counting against the spoken side would let a heavier dial shrink
  its own denominator, so "the the report" would read as one repetition per four
  words rather than per three.
- 2026-09-08: The first bucketing cut `d1` as "filled pauses and nothing else"
  and `d2` as "carries a repetition or a substitution", following the brief.
  Measured, that gives `d1` a filler rate of 10.7 per 100 words against `d2`'s
  3.6: the first definition guarantees a filler and the second does not, so
  turning the dial from light to natural would have *removed* hesitations. It
  was not a length artefact, and held at 8.9 against 3.9 when both buckets were
  restricted to utterances of 10 to 25 words. Discarded.
- 2026-09-08: Buckets are quantiles of the total rate instead: `d0` zero, `d1`
  below the median disfluent utterance, `d2` median to 90th percentile, `d3`
  above. Monotone in all four types by construction, which
  `test_levels.py::test_every_type_rate_rises_at_every_step` now holds it to.
- 2026-09-08: Only utterances of at least 10 clean words are measured or trained
  on. Switchboard's short slash units are backchannels, and a rate computed over
  five words is quantised so coarsely that one filled pause reads as 20 events
  per 100 words. Costs 46,000 of the 81,107 utterances and leaves 35,208.
- 2026-09-08: The brief specified `d1` as fillers only at about one per 20
  words. The corpus says the lightest half of disfluent Switchboard is 58% filled
  pauses at one per 26 words. The measurement is what the README reports.
- 2026-09-08: `hem/levels.json` is generated by `python -m hem.rates` and checked
  in, so importing the dial needs no corpus and CI can test it.

## The injector

- 2026-09-08: Mend's injector takes per-sentence probabilities. Hem's takes rates
  per 100 words and draws a count from them, because rate control is a scored
  axis and a per-sentence probability cannot hit a per-word target on long and
  short sentences at once.
- 2026-09-08: `draw_count` takes the integer part of the expected count and adds
  one more with probability equal to the remainder, rather than rounding.
  Rounding kills the dial below twenty words: at 3 events per 100 words a
  twelve-word sentence expects 0.36 and would always come back clean.
- 2026-09-08: Mend's injector could only place a repair on a word from a closed
  category (a name, a day, a colour). Most scripted text names none, so Hem also
  allows the span-drop repair, where the first attempt is a shorter version of
  the phrase, which needs no word list.
- 2026-09-08: Filled pauses are drawn with Switchboard's own weights (uh 0.445,
  you know 0.256, well 0.097, um 0.090) rather than uniformly. Uniform draws
  produce far too many instances of *like*, which is the one a reader notices.
- 2026-09-08: `interregnum_rate`, `span_repair_rate` and
  `repetition_bigram_rate` are measured off the corpus. `delayed_repair_rate` and
  `span_drop_rate` are not, and are kept at Mend's values: the parser closes a
  reparandum over the minimum and maximum of the words it covers, so a gap
  between the two halves of a repair is not recoverable from it.
- 2026-09-08: The injector keeps Mend's filler placement rule (clause onset or
  in front of a content word), so the baseline is not a strawman on the axis
  Shriberg names. It still loses badly there, which is the finding.

## Training

- 2026-09-08: The control token is added to the tokenizer as a special token and
  the embedding matrix resized. As plain text `<d2>` tokenises to four pieces
  that share three of them with the other three settings.
- 2026-09-08: Every training pair is labelled by its own measured rate, not by
  the setting it was generated at. The injector is stochastic, so a sentence
  asked for at `d3` sometimes lands at `d2`, and keeping the asked-for label
  would teach the model that `d3` sometimes means light.
- 2026-09-08: That relabelling skews the mix towards the lower buckets, because a
  short sentence at a heavy setting falls to a lower bucket and never the
  reverse. `make_synthetic` corrects it by aiming each next draw at the emptiest
  measured bucket, which gets the synthetic split to 12,500 pairs per level.
- 2026-09-08: Greedy decoding, no sampling. Sampling would let the rate be tuned
  after the fact with the temperature, and the point of the control token is that
  the rate is something the model learned.
- 2026-09-09: **That was wrong and is reversed.** Greedy collapses the dial:
  measured over 1,200 held-out scripts it reaches 1.77, 2.34 and 6.17 events per
  100 words against targets of 6.67, 14.20 and 28.06. The reason is not a bug.
  The model is a distribution over ways of saying a sentence and the most likely
  way to say any sentence is fluently, because a filled pause has to go somewhere
  and no one position carries as much probability as inserting nothing. Ancestral
  sampling at temperature 1, with `top_k` and `top_p` off, reaches 4.93, 11.09
  and 22.44. Temperature 1 is not a tuned value, it is the absence of one, and
  temperature 1.3 is why it stays there: it overshoots the targets and takes the
  script loss from 4.4% to 16.9% at the natural setting. Sampling is seeded, so
  the numbers still reproduce. The greedily decoded model is kept as its own row
  in every table.
- 2026-09-08: Mixed 50k synthetic with 24,619 real pairs (33% real), matching
  Mend's best configuration, so the two are comparable.
- 2026-09-08: The first full run degraded from 1.5 s/step to 25 s/step and drove
  the machine into swap. Cause was Mend's documented MPS problem, the allocator
  keeping a pool per tensor shape and never releasing it, made worse by running
  the test suite alongside training. MPS allocations do not appear in RSS, so the
  training process showed 137 MB while thrashing. Fixed by trimming the MPS cache
  every 25 steps rather than every 100, and by not running anything else
  alongside it. Step time went back to 1.38 s and stayed there.

## The detector

- 2026-09-08: Model output carries no annotation, so every rate, mix and
  placement number needs a detector that recovers insertions from the clean text
  and the disfluent text alone. It is the single most load-bearing piece in the
  repo.
- 2026-09-08: The detector is scored against Switchboard's own spans rather than
  trusted: `python -m hem.detect --check` reports detection recall 0.928,
  precision 0.876 and type accuracy 0.879 of the events it matched, on 4,000
  held-out utterances. Every table that the detector produced carries that
  number nearby.
- 2026-09-08: Switchboard is reported through the detector *and* from its gold
  spans in the type-mix table, so the gap between the two rows is the detector's
  own error and is the floor any system is really being measured against.
- 2026-09-08: A `replace` alignment opcode is split into a delete and an insert,
  so the inserted side reaches the classifier and the deleted side is reported as
  lost script.
- 2026-09-08: Filler phrases are peeled off both ends of an inserted run before
  the rest is classified, so "uh john no wait" scores as a filled pause plus a
  substitution rather than as one thing in the wrong bucket.
- 2026-09-08: Tried extending repetition detection to runs matching a prefix of
  what follows, to catch "that's some that's something". Type accuracy fell from
  0.879 to 0.868. Reverted.
- 2026-09-08: Restarts are not reliably detectable. Switchboard's are mostly
  single cut-off words ("wh", "ele", "becau"), and Mend's parser strips the
  trailing hyphen while building the tokens, so they arrive indistinguishable
  from ordinary words. The detector finds 1 of 83. The parser was left alone
  rather than fixed, because changing it would put fragments into Hem's output
  that the shipped Mend model has never seen and would break the round trip.
  Reported as a limitation instead.
- 2026-09-08: A delayed repair emits its corrected word out of clean order, which
  the aligner reads as a deletion plus an insertion. On the injector's own
  heaviest output, where nothing is ever really deleted, this misfires on under
  5% of sentences.

## Evaluation

- 2026-09-08: Every system, including the Switchboard reference, is counted by
  the same detector. Scoring the corpus from its gold spans and the models from a
  detector would have made the comparison meaningless.
- 2026-09-08: The placement baseline is computed per corpus, not once. Switchboard
  transcribers comma far more heavily than the scripted sentences do, and a raw
  clause-onset share compared across the two measures a transcriber's punctuation
  rather than a speaker's hesitations.
- 2026-09-08: The Shriberg prediction about low-frequency words is reported
  mid-clause only. Asked of all filled pauses the answer is confounded: most sit
  at clause onsets, and the word after a clause onset is nearly always a very
  common one, so a system that gets the onset behaviour right looks like it
  prefers easy words.
- 2026-09-08: The surprisal figure plots the paired difference from each
  instance's own fluent control, not raw surprisal. Telephone conversation and
  WikiText prose differ by bits before any disfluency is inserted, and a raw plot
  would show that instead.
- 2026-09-08: The round trip reports recovery up to casing and punctuation as the
  headline. Mend restores those from its own training distribution, and a comma
  landing somewhere Switchboard would not put it is not a failure of the round
  trip.
- 2026-09-08: KL is smoothed on the reference side. A system that never produces
  restarts would otherwise score an infinite divergence off one empty cell.
- 2026-09-09: The placement baseline was computed once, over the scripted
  sentences, and applied to the Switchboard row too. That made Switchboard's
  clause-onset share look like a 7x lift when its own text puts the figure at
  5.5x. Fixed, and the earlier version is noted in the README rather than quietly
  replaced.
- 2026-09-09: Added a script-fidelity table. No rate metric can tell a generator
  that hits its target by inserting from one that hits it by deleting, and the
  model does the second more than expected: at the natural setting 47% of
  sentences lose at least one word of the script. The injector keeps 1.000 by
  construction, which is the comparison that makes the number readable.
- 2026-09-09: Added `hem/copyfail.py` after the qualitative table showed the
  model mangling rare proper nouns, which the detector counts as substitutions.
  Splitting the scripts on whether they contain a word absent from Switchboard
  shows the effect is real (5.77 against 3.91) and partial: the excess over
  target survives on scripts made only of words Switchboard says. The injector's
  rate does not move across the split, which is the control that makes the test
  mean anything.
- 2026-09-09: The round trip is `python -m hem.roundtrip`, a separate process.
  Loading Mend inside `hem.evaluate`, with two Hem checkpoints and their MPS
  decode buffers still resident, got the process killed by the system twice with
  no traceback. The tables are also written before the round trip runs, so a
  failure there cannot take the rest of the evaluation with it.
- 2026-09-09: The temperature comparison was first run on a 150-sentence probe
  and put in a table next to numbers from the 1,200-sentence evaluation, where
  the greedy rows disagreed by a factor of two on sample variation alone. Re-run
  on the same 1,200 scripts before publishing.
- 2026-09-09: The evaluation reports the injector as the better system on rate
  control, script fidelity, the round trip and type mix at two settings of three.
  The model wins on placement, which is the axis it was predicted to win on, and
  nowhere else. Reported that way round.
